Images do not know what is behind them. A white screenshot on a white card has no edge at all, and a photo with a bright corner blends into the page. An **inset outline** fixes that with one line, drawn just inside the image, at around 10% opacity. Toggle it on below and look at the corners of each tile.

<ImageOutlineDemo />

The line is too faint to read as a border. What you notice instead is that every image suddenly has a shape. On the dark tile it barely shows, on the photo it tidies the corners, and on the white screenshot it is the only thing separating the image from the card.

## Why not a border

A `border` sits between the padding and the margin, so it takes up space. Adding one pushes content around and changes the size of the box. It also has to match the image's `border-radius` exactly, and a 1px border on a rounded image tends to show a thin sliver of background at the corners.

An `outline` with a negative `outline-offset`, or an inset `box-shadow`, paints on top of the image instead. Nothing moves, nothing resizes, and the line follows the corner radius for free. Because it is painted over the image, it also works on images you do not control, like user uploads and favicons.

## How strong

Ten percent is a good default. Drag the slider to see what happens on either side of it.

<ImageOutlineStrengthDemo />

Below about 5% the line disappears on the white screenshot. Above about 20% it starts to look like a frame, and the eye reads it as a design element instead of a fix. Stay in between.

## Avatars

Avatars are where this matters most. They are small, round, and often mostly white: an initial on a light background, a pale logo, a photo of someone against a wall. Without an edge they float on the row. Toggle the outline and watch the top one.

<ImageOutlineAvatarDemo />

The same rule fixes all three at once, no matter what is inside. That is the point of doing it in CSS instead of editing the image.

## Usage

Set the outline color per theme. Black at 10% on light, white at 10% on dark.

<CodeBlock
  tabs={[
    {
      label: "Tailwind",
      language: "html",
      icon: "tailwind",
      code: `<img
  class="rounded-lg outline-1 -outline-offset-1 outline-black/10 dark:outline-white/10"
  src="/cover.jpg"
  alt="Album cover"
/>`,
    },
    {
      label: "CSS",
      language: "css",
      icon: "css",
      code: `img {
  border-radius: 8px;
  outline: 1px solid rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1);
  outline-offset: -1px;
}

.dark img {
  outline-color: rgb(255 255 255 / 0.1);
}`,
    },
  ]}
/>

Keep it off images that already have a hard edge, like icons drawn on a transparent background. The outline only helps when the image fills its box. A quick way to check: put a screenshot on a card of the same color and see if you can still tell where it ends.
