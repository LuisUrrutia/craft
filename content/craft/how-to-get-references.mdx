If all your references are other interfaces, your work drifts toward the
average of everything on Dribbble. The designers whose work feels like it came
from somewhere are pulling from outside the discipline, and they do it on
purpose.

References are inputs. You cannot get out more than you put in.

## Where the good ones look

**Film.** Editors solved "guide the eye through a change" a century before we
had transitions. Depth of field, staging, cutting on action. Rauno's essay on
depth is basically a film grammar lesson applied to layers and blur.

Here is one of those ideas, translated. A dialog opens over some content.

Flat just dims. Depth pushes the content back, softens it, and lets the dialog
sit in front the way a face sits in front of a blurred street. Nothing about
that is native to screens. It was borrowed.

**Architecture.** Buildings are interfaces you walk through. Thresholds are
onboarding. Sight lines are hierarchy. Material honesty, where wood is allowed
to look like wood, is the same rule as not styling a link to look like a
button.

**Print and type.** Grids, rhythm, and restraint were worked out on paper long
before screens. A lot of why Vercel looks like Vercel is Swiss typography.

**Music.** Easing is phrasing. Stagger is rhythm. A success sound is a cadence.
The copy sound on this site is a rising arpeggio because music already knew
what "done" sounds like.

**Physical things.** Drag gestures feel right when they have momentum because
Apple copied the physics of real objects. Pick up a well made tool and pay
attention to how it stops moving.

## Make it a practice

Two habits separate people who collect references from people who use them.

**Capture them.** A folder, an Are.na channel, a camera roll full of door
handles and ticket stubs. Taste is trained, and this is the training data.

**Translate, do not transplant.** Ask what a reference does. How it directs
attention, how it builds and releases tension, what it leaves out. Then rebuild
that mechanism in your medium. Paco calls remixing a legitimate creative act,
and it is, as long as you remix the mechanism and not the pixels.

The test for a good reference is whether you can explain what it taught you
without showing it to anyone.
